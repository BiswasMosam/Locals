# Interview guide

How to turn a profile's guesses into facts. Thirty to forty five minutes, at their place of work if possible, in whatever language they think in.

## Before

- Ask permission, and say why: "I'm building something that tests apps the way people like you use them. I want to get your day right."
- Promise them their name will not be written anywhere. Keep that promise: notes go in `study/interviews/`, which is never committed.
- Read the profile's open questions first. Bring them, but don't read them out like a form.

## The rules

1. **Ask about yesterday, not "usually".** "Walk me through yesterday from when you opened the shop" gets real answers. "What do you usually do" gets the version they wish were true.
2. **Ask to be shown.** "Can you show me the last order you wrote down?" beats any question. Photograph the diary page, the WhatsApp chat, the screen, with permission.
3. **Never ask what features they want.** Ask what went wrong last time, and what they did instead. The workaround is the feature.
4. **Ask for the worst day.** "Tell me about a day when something got missed." Stakes come out in stories.
5. **Stay quiet after they answer.** The useful sentence usually comes after the pause.

## Questions

**The day**
- Walk me through yesterday, from the first work call to the last.
- When during the day is your phone in your hand? When is it impossible to use?
- What interrupts you most?

**The work**
- What happens between a customer's first call and the money arriving? Every step.
- Where does each piece of information live? Paper, phone, memory, someone else?
- What gets forgotten? What did it cost the last time?

**The tools**
- Show me the last thing you wrote down for work.
- What do you use WhatsApp for at work?
- Have you tried an app for this before? What happened to it?

**The phone**
- Which phone, how old, how full? (Look at it, with permission.)
- Do you type, use voice notes, or call?
- When something on the phone is confusing, who do you ask?

**Seasons**
- Which months are busiest? Which are dead? Why?

## Watch them use the app

If they already use one of our apps, ask them to do one real task while you watch, saying nothing. Write down:

- Where they paused, and for how long
- What they tapped first
- What they read and what they skipped
- What they said out loud
- Whether they finished, and how they knew they had

These notes are the yardstick a local must match. If a local finishes a task that a real person could not, the local is wrong, not the person.

## After

- Within a day, go through the profile: turn each confirmed `[G]` into `[I]` or `[O]`, delete what was wrong, add what was missing.
- Write down anything that surprised you. Surprises are where the profile was weakest.
