# In-house counsel

**Status:** draft from published sources and job descriptions. Family context in [Law](README.md).

## 1. Snapshot

A lawyer employed by a company rather than a firm or court practice, serving colleagues in sales, HR, finance and operations `[A]` (job descriptions). Much of the work is drafting, reviewing and negotiating contracts in volume `[A]`; a global survey put the average in-house lawyer at 4.5 hours a day on contract review `[D]` (vendor run, not India specific). A good day is every request answered without blocking the business. A bad day is a signed contract nobody can find, or a renewal deadline that passed `[G]`.

## 3. The week, the month, the year

- Quarter end: sales pushes to sign deals `[G]`.
- Statutory filing deadlines and board meetings: the board must meet at least four times a year, no more than 120 days apart (Companies Act 2013, section 173), the financial statements go to the registrar within 30 days of the annual general meeting and the annual return within 60 (sections 137 and 92) `[D]`.
- Contract renewals scattered across the year `[G]`.

## 9. Task cards

| Task | Pressure | Done when |
|---|---|---|
| Review an NDA from sales on the phone and approve it with two changes. | Between meetings, sales waiting | Comments sent and approval recorded |
| Find the signed version of a vendor contract from two years ago. | Finance needs it now | The executed copy is open |
| Set a reminder 90 days before a contract's notice deadline. | Easy to forget | The reminder exists for the right date |
| Answer a colleague's quick legal question with a saved standard answer. | Ten questions waiting | The answer is sent |
| Fill a vendor onboarding form with the company's legal details. | Blocks a purchase | The form is submitted correctly |

## 10. Limits in numbers

Not set yet. To measure: how long a phone review lasts before they defer it to the laptop, how many steps they tolerate to find a document.

## 11. Caricature traps

- **"The department of no."** Their goal is to get the business to yes safely.
- **Endless legal detail in narration.** They speak in short, practical answers to colleagues.

## 12. Open questions

1. Walk through yesterday from the first request.
2. Where do requests arrive, and how do you track them?
3. Where do signed contracts live, and how long does finding one take?
4. Which deadlines have you nearly missed?
5. What do you review on the phone, and what never?
6. Which tools did the company give you, and which do you avoid?
7. How do you work with outside counsel?
