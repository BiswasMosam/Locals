# Health

People who look after the town's health, split into five types by **how differently their day runs**: a clinic with a queue outside, a hospital duty that does not end at night, a ward on shifts, a chemist's counter, and a community health worker walking house to house. Specialities become the different people inside a type.

| Type | Where the day happens | What rules it | Profile |
|---|---|---|---|
| Clinic doctor | A neighbourhood clinic, morning and evening | The queue, about two minutes a patient | [Draft](clinic-doctor.md) |
| Resident doctor | A teaching hospital's wards and casualty | Duty rosters with stretches past 36 hours | [Draft](resident-doctor.md) |
| Staff nurse | A hospital ward or ICU, in shifts | Too many patients per nurse, and the handover | [Draft](staff-nurse.md) |
| Pharmacist | A chemist's counter | Prescriptions, stock and the next customer | [Draft](pharmacist.md) |
| ASHA worker | Homes in her area, on foot | Visits, registers and many apps | [Draft](asha-worker.md) |

## What the family shares

- **Very little time per person.** A review of 67 countries put the average primary care consultation in India at about two minutes `[D]`.
- **Too few people.** A CAG audit found a 35% shortage of nurses in Maharashtra's public health facilities, with 37% of doctor and 44% of paramedic posts short `[D]`.
- **The monsoon fills the wards.** BMC's own figures for January to August 2025 counted 4,825 malaria cases and 1,564 dengue cases in Mumbai, and the city adds fever beds every monsoon `[D]`.
- **Paper first, then the app.** ASHAs write in a paper diary and then enter the same details into several apps `[A]`; a union leader said they have "as many apps as there are diseases" `[A]`.
