# Clinic doctor

**Status:** draft. Family context in [Health](README.md).

## 1. Snapshot
A general practitioner with a neighbourhood clinic, open in the morning and again in the evening `[G]`. Primary care consultations in India average about two minutes `[D]`. A good session is the queue cleared on time. A bad one is a monsoon evening with every bench full `[D]` `[G]`.

## 2. The day
| Time | Where | Doing | Hands and eyes | Phone use | Interruptions |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Morning | Clinic | OPD: one patient after another `[G]` | Stethoscope, prescription pad `[G]` | Between patients only `[G]` | The next name called `[G]` |
| Afternoon | Home or hospital visits | Rest, a hospital round `[G]` | Phone `[G]` | Reports sent by patients `[G]` | Calls from patients `[G]` |
| Evening | Clinic | OPD again, the longer queue `[G]` | Desk, sometimes a laptop `[G]` | Lab reports on WhatsApp `[G]` | Emergencies walking in `[G]` |

## 3. The year
Monsoon fevers in July to September are the busiest months `[D]`; winter brings coughs and colds `[G]`; Sundays are a half day `[G]`.

## 4. Tools today
Prescription pads, sometimes clinic software, WhatsApp for reports and follow ups `[G]`, and a phone for teleconsults, which the Telemedicine Practice Guidelines of March 2020 allow a registered doctor to give `[D]`. Since 2016 the conduct rules ask for generic names written legibly, preferably in capitals `[D]`.

## 5. Phone and ability
Skilled and quick, with no time to spare; reads the first words and decides `[G]`.

## 6. Pressures
The queue; patients who want to be seen first; reports that come as blurry photos `[G]`.
