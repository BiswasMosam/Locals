# Fabrication shop owner

**Status:** draft. Built from what we learned making [Pops](https://github.com/BiswasMosam/Pops) for a real fabrication business, plus published data. Every `[G]` waits for an interview.

## 1. Snapshot

Runs a small shop that makes and fits grills, invisible grills, mosquito nets, sliding windows, safety doors, gates and blinds for flats in housing societies `[P]`. Work comes in by phone and word of mouth, goes out to a site visit for measurements, comes back as a handwritten quotation, and turns into money only if the customer says yes and someone follows up `[P]`. A good day is several visits quoted and one job fitted. A bad day is a customer who calls back angry because nobody came, or a quote that was forgotten and went to a competitor `[P]`.

## 2. The day

Shop hours are about 9 to 7 on weekdays and shorter on Sunday `[P]`. Everything inside those hours below is a draft.

| Time | Where | Doing | Hands and eyes | Phone use | Interruptions |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 9:00 | Shop | Opens, assigns the day's fitting jobs to workers `[G]` | Free | Calls to workers `[G]` | Customers calling about yesterday `[G]` |
| 10:30 | On the road and at sites | Site visits to measure `[P]` | Tape measure, diary, often standing on a ladder or at a window `[G]` | Calls answered while travelling `[G]` | New leads call mid visit `[G]` |
| 14:00 | Fitting sites | Checks on installations `[G]` | Dirty hands, noise, sun `[G]` | Photos of finished work `[G]` | Society watchmen, residents `[G]` |
| 17:00 | Shop | Writes quotations in the diary `[P]` | Seated, both hands free `[G]` | Sends quote photos on WhatsApp `[G]` | Walk in customers `[G]` |
| 19:00 | Home | Follow up calls, if remembered `[P]` | Tired `[G]` | Payments arrive by UPI `[P]` | Family `[G]` |

## 3. The week, the month, the year

- Monsoon raises demand for mosquito nets and monsoon blinds `[G]`.
- New township buildings getting possession bring many similar jobs at once, flat after flat `[G]`.
- Festival season, before Diwali, brings renovation work `[G]`.

## 4. Tools today

| Tool | What it is good at |
|---|---|
| Paper diary `[P]` | Instant, never runs out of battery, quotes can be sketched with dimensions |
| Photo of the diary page `[P]` | Shares a quote on WhatsApp without retyping it |
| WhatsApp `[D]` | Where customers already are; 97% of surveyed small businesses use it |
| UPI `[D]` | Advance and final payment; a screenshot is the receipt `[P]` |
| Memory `[G]` | Knows regular societies and watchmen by name |

The diary's weakness is not writing, it is finding: which quotes are waiting, who needs a follow up, what was quoted for that flat last year `[P]`.

## 5. Phone and ability

- Android phone, used all day for calls, WhatsApp and UPI `[D]` `[G]`.
- Comfortable with calling, photos and WhatsApp; slower typing long text `[G]`.
- Reads trade words in English (quotation, advance, sq ft) but may think in another language `[G]`.
- A family member set up any new app `[P]`.

## 6. Pressures

- A forgotten follow up is a lost job `[P]`.
- A customer on the line asking "what did you quote me?" needs an answer in seconds `[G]`.
- Money owed after fitting has to be chased `[G]`.

## 9. Task cards

These map to what Pops does today `[P]`.

| Task | Pressure | Done when |
|---|---|---|
| A new customer calls during a site visit. Add them so they are not forgotten. | One free hand, customer still talking | Order sits in To Visit with the right society, building and flat |
| Evening: attach photos of today's three diary quotes to the right customers. | Tired, end of day | Three orders moved to Given Quotation, each with the right photo |
| A customer calls: "what did you quote me last week?" | Customer waiting on the line | Quote photo on screen within thirty seconds |
| The customer paid the advance by UPI. Record it. | Wants to get back to work | Advance amount and screenshot saved on the order |
| A resident of a flat you fitted last year calls again. Find the old job. | Wants to sound like he remembers | Past orders for that flat found from the address |

## 11. Caricature traps

- **"Older shop owners can't use phones."** Wrong. They run their business on calls, WhatsApp and UPI every day `[D]`. The limits are time, free hands and context, not ability.
- **Writing their thoughts in broken English.** Their reasoning is sharp. A local's narration should be plain, not mocking.
- **Wanting dashboards and analytics.** Their questions are concrete: who is waiting, what did I quote, who owes me.

## 12. Open questions

1. What does a typical day really look like, hour by hour?
2. How do new leads arrive: calls, walk ins, WhatsApp, referrals? Which most?
3. What is in the hands during a site visit, and is the phone used there at all?
4. Who writes the quote, and when: at the site or back at the shop?
5. Which months are busy and why?
6. How often does a customer call back asking about an old quote?
7. What was the last job that was lost because of a missed follow up?
8. Which language is used for thinking and for typing?
9. What happens when a customer pays: who checks it, where is it recorded?
10. Has any other app been tried for this? What happened to it?
